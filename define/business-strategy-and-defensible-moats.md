# Loom Business Strategy & Defensible Moats

This document analyzes Loom's business strategy from two critical angles:
1. **Go-to-Market Strategy**: How to position Loom for maximum market impact
2. **Defensible Position**: Where human intelligence creates long-term competitive moats

---

## 1. Go-to-Market Strategy: AI Revolution Participation

### Question
Can Loom be positioned as enabling companies to participate in the AI revolution by delivering:
- Custom software at a fraction of traditional cost and time
- Solutions tailored to company-specific pain points
- Company-specific process support
- With human expert guidance for design and ROI calculation

### Answer: YES - This is a STRONGER value proposition than the original positioning

### Value Proposition: Company-Specific Solutions

Traditional software has 3 options, all flawed:

#### Option 1: Off-the-Shelf Software
- **Pro**: Fast, cheap
- **Con**: Generic, doesn't fit company-specific needs, forces process changes
- **Example**: Buying SAP, must adapt processes to SAP's way

#### Option 2: Customizable Platforms
- **Pro**: Some flexibility
- **Con**: Limited to platform capabilities, expensive customization
- **Example**: Salesforce + custom development = still expensive

#### Option 3: Fully Custom Software (Traditional)
- **Pro**: Perfectly tailored
- **Con**: Extremely expensive ($500K-$2M), slow (6-12 months), high risk
- **Example**: Hiring a software agency

#### Loom Option 4: AI-Powered Custom Software ⭐
- **Pro**: Perfectly tailored + Fast + Affordable
- **Cost**: $50K-$200K (vs $500K-$2M)
- **Time**: 4-8 weeks (vs 6-12 months)
- **Result**: Company-specific solution at platform prices

**How it works:**

1. **Domain Vocabulary (L0)** = Company's language
   - Company-specific terms, definitions
   - Industry jargon
   - Internal terminology

2. **User Stories (L0)** = Company's processes
   - Exact workflows
   - Specific pain points
   - Unique requirements

3. **Business Rules (L1)** = Company's policies
   - Company-specific rules
   - Compliance requirements
   - Custom logic

4. **AI Derivation (L1, L2, L3)** = Automated translation
   - Domain model
   - Interface contracts
   - Test cases
   - Implementation

**Result**: Software that speaks the company's language, follows company's processes, implements company's rules.

### The Human Expert's Role (Critical Differentiator)

The human Loom expert provides value AI cannot:

#### 1. Discovery & Problem Framing (2-5 days, $5K-$15K)

**Activities:**
- Interview stakeholders
- Identify real pain points (not just stated problems)
- Map current processes
- Uncover hidden inefficiencies
- Calculate true costs of current state

**Example:**
```
Client says: "We need a better CRM"

Junior consultant: Builds CRM → $100K, doesn't solve problem

Senior Loom expert discovers:
- Real problem: Sales and Operations don't communicate
- Root cause: No shared system of record
- True cost: $500K/year in lost deals, duplicated work
- Better solution: Lightweight workflow tool + integration
- Cost: $60K (vs $100K CRM)
- ROI: $440K/year savings

→ Human expertise saves client $40K upfront + $440K/year
```

**Deliverable:** Discovery report with:
- Pain point analysis
- Root cause identification
- Proposed solution architecture (L0 sketch)
- ROI calculation
- Implementation roadmap

#### 2. Solution Design & Architecture (3-5 days, included in implementation)

**Activities:**
- Design L0 documents (domain vocabulary, user stories)
- Define acceptance criteria
- Make architecture decisions
- Select technology stack
- Plan integration points

**AI cannot do this because:**
- Requires deep domain expertise
- Requires understanding company culture, capabilities
- Requires strategic thinking
- Requires creative problem solving
- Requires trade-off decisions (speed vs quality, build vs buy)

**Example:**
```
Company: Mid-size manufacturer
Pain: Manual inventory management
- 20% over-ordering (tied up capital)
- 15% under-ordering (lost sales)
- 5 FTEs managing spreadsheets

Loom expert designs:
- Predictive inventory system
- Integrates with existing ERP
- ML-based demand forecasting
- Automated reordering
- Dashboard for managers

L0 documents (2 days):
- Domain vocabulary: SKU, lead time, safety stock, etc.
- User stories: "As warehouse manager, I want to see predicted stockouts"
- Acceptance criteria: "System predicts stockouts 7 days in advance with 90% accuracy"

AI derives rest (3 weeks):
- Domain model
- Interface contracts
- Test cases (TDAI)
- Implementation
```

#### 3. ROI Calculation & Business Case (1 day, part of discovery)

**Framework:**

```
Current State Costs:
- Labor costs (FTEs × $80K/year)
- Error costs (over-ordering, under-ordering, rework)
- Opportunity costs (lost sales, delayed decisions)
- System maintenance costs
Total: $A/year

Loom Implementation:
- Upfront: $B (one-time)
- Annual: $C (support, hosting)

Savings:
- Labor reduction: -$D/year (automation)
- Error reduction: -$E/year (quality)
- Opportunity gains: +$F/year (new capabilities)
Total annual benefit: $G/year

ROI = (G - C) / B × 100%
Payback period = B / (G - C) months

Example:
Current costs: $800K/year
Implementation: $120K
Annual support: $20K
Savings: $500K/year (labor) + $300K/year (errors)
Annual benefit: $800K - $20K = $780K
ROI: 650% first year
Payback: 1.8 months ⚡
```

**This is what sells the project!**

#### 4. AI Orchestration & Quality Assurance (2-4 weeks, during implementation)

**Human reviews:**
- L1 derivation (domain model, business rules)
- L2 derivation (architecture, interfaces)
- L3 derivation (test cases)
- Implementation quality
- Traceability completeness

**AI cannot do this because:**

#### 5. Deployment, Training & Change Management (1-2 weeks)

**Activities:**
- Deploy to company infrastructure
- Train team on new system
- Handle change resistance
- Create documentation
- Set up support

**AI cannot do this because:**
- Requires human relationships, trust
- Requires understanding company politics
- Requires coaching, mentoring
- Requires empathy, patience

## 2. Defensible Human Intelligence Moats

### Question
Where does human intelligence enter the system, and where is it irreplaceable by AI (medium to long term)? These are the points we must focus on for business planning to maintain our position.

### Answer: Three Critical Layers of Defensible Human Value

---

### Layer 1: L0 Document Creation (MOST DEFENSIBLE)

This is where AI **cannot** replace humans, even in 5-10 years:

#### 1.1 Deep Domain Expertise

**What AI cannot do:**
```
❌ Understand company-specific business context
❌ Recognize implicit knowledge (unstated rules, tribal knowledge)
❌ Navigate company politics and power structures
❌ Build trust with stakeholders
❌ Identify real pain points (vs stated problems)
❌ Understand "why" behind processes
```

**Human expertise required:**
- Years of experience in specific industry (manufacturing, logistics, healthcare)
- Understanding of business economics
- Knowledge of regulatory environment
- Relationships with industry experts
- Pattern recognition across companies

**Example:**
```
AI sees: "Manual inventory process"
AI thinks: "Build inventory management software"

Human expert sees: "Manual inventory process"
Human discovers:
- Root cause: Supplier lead times unpredictable
- Hidden cost: Warehouse holding costs $50K/month
- Opportunity: Negotiate supplier contracts for predictability
- Better solution: Supplier integration + smaller safety stock
- Cheaper, faster, higher ROI than inventory software

→ AI builds software
→ Human solves business problem
```

**Why defensible:**
- Requires 5-10 years domain experience
- Requires business acumen, not just technical skill
- Requires emotional intelligence (stakeholder management)
- Cannot be learned from documentation alone
- Relationship-based (trust, credibility)

#### 1.2 Business Problem Framing

**What AI cannot do:**
```
❌ Frame the right problem to solve
❌ Question stated requirements
❌ Identify what's NOT being said
❌ Prioritize conflicting goals
❌ Make strategic trade-offs
❌ Think "outside the box"
```

**Human expertise required:**
- Critical thinking
- Socratic questioning
- Business strategy understanding
- Ability to challenge assumptions
- Creative problem solving

**Example:**
```
Client request: "We need a mobile app for field service"

Junior developer (or AI):
→ Builds mobile app ($100K, 3 months)
→ Adoption: 20% (field workers resist)
→ ROI: Negative

Senior Loom expert:
→ Interviews field workers
→ Discovers: They already use WhatsApp for everything
→ Real problem: Office doesn't have visibility into field status
→ Solution: WhatsApp Business API integration ($20K, 2 weeks)
→ Adoption: 100% (workers already using WhatsApp)
→ ROI: 5x better than mobile app

→ AI solves stated problem
→ Human identifies real problem
```

**Why defensible:**
- Requires business judgment (not algorithmic)
- Requires ability to challenge client
- Requires experience (pattern matching across projects)
- Requires empathy (understanding user resistance)

#### 1.3 Solution Design & Architecture

**What AI cannot do:**
```
❌ Design innovative, creative solutions
❌ Make architecture trade-offs (speed vs cost vs quality)
❌ Consider company capabilities and constraints
❌ Design for future flexibility
❌ Take accountability for decisions
```

**Human expertise required:**
- Architectural thinking
- Trade-off analysis
- Risk assessment
- Long-term planning
- Technology selection

**Example:**
```
Problem: E-commerce company wants faster checkout

AI solution:
- One-click checkout
- Stored payment methods
- Auto-fill forms
→ Standard solution

Human expert discovers:
- Company sells B2B ($10K+ orders)
- Real problem: Procurement approval workflows
- Need: Multi-level approval automation
- Solution: Approval workflow engine + Slack integration
→ Different solution, higher impact

→ AI gives generic solution
→ Human gives company-specific solution
```

**Why defensible:**
- Requires creativity (not just pattern matching)
- Requires accountability (AI doesn't take responsibility)
- Requires strategic thinking
- Requires understanding of constraints (budget, timeline, team skills)

### Layer 2: Review & Validation Points (DEFENSIBLE)

AI generates L1, L2, L3, but humans must review. Why?

#### 2.1 L1 Derivation Review (Domain Model, Business Rules)

**What AI cannot do:**
```
❌ Validate its own understanding
❌ Catch subtle domain misinterpretations
❌ Verify alignment with business strategy
❌ Identify missing edge cases
❌ Judge reasonableness of rules
```

**Example:**
```
AI generates business rule:
"Discount ≤ 20% for all orders"

Human expert catches:
- Company policy: Regional managers can approve up to 30%
- Company policy: VIP customers get automatic 25%
- Company policy: End-of-quarter promotions up to 40%

→ AI misunderstood from incomplete L0
→ Human catches before implementation (saves 1 week)
```

**Why defensible:**
- Requires implicit knowledge
- Requires business judgment
- AI cannot validate itself (fundamental limitation)

#### 2.2 Architecture & Technology Decisions

**What AI cannot do:**
```
❌ Make trade-off decisions with full context
❌ Evaluate long-term implications
❌ Consider company's technical debt and constraints
❌ Select technologies based on team capabilities
❌ Take responsibility for consequences
```

**Example:**
```
AI recommends: Microservices architecture

Human expert evaluates:
- Team: 2 developers, no DevOps
- Current: Monolith on Heroku
- Budget: Limited
- Timeline: 6 weeks

Human decides: Enhanced monolith (not microservices)
→ Faster delivery
→ Lower operational complexity
→ Better fit for team

→ AI recommends "best practice"
→ Human recommends "best for context"
```

**Why defensible:**
- Requires holistic understanding
- Requires accountability
- Requires experience with failures (not just successes)

#### 2.3 TDAI Test Plan Review

**What AI cannot do:**
```
❌ Identify business-critical edge cases
❌ Understand which bugs are "critical" vs "nice to fix"
❌ Prioritize test coverage based on risk
❌ Guarantee test completeness
```

**Example:**
```
AI generates 10 tests for payment processing

Human expert adds:
- Test: Concurrent payment attempts (race condition)
- Test: Payment timeout during processing
- Test: Partial refund edge cases
- Test: Currency conversion edge cases

→ AI covers happy path + basic errors
→ Human adds business-critical edge cases
```

**Why defensible:**
- Requires deep domain knowledge (what can go wrong)
- Requires risk assessment (what matters most)
- Requires experience (seen production failures)

## AI Evolution Scenarios

### Scenario 1: AI Gets Much Better (Most Likely)

**What changes:**
- AI can write better L1/L2/L3 derivations
- AI can implement more complex systems
- AI can suggest optimizations

**What doesn't change:**
- L0 still requires human domain expertise
- Review still requires human judgment
- ROI calculation still requires business expertise
- Change management still requires human touch
- Strategic advisory still requires creativity

**Our response:**
- Humans focus more on L0 and advisory
- Price drops on implementation (AI does more)
- Price increases on consulting (more value)
- Net: Higher margins (less human time per project)

---

### Scenario 2: AI Can Do L0 (Unlikely in 5 years)

**What changes:**
- AI can interview stakeholders
- AI can write initial L0 documents
- AI can suggest solutions

**What doesn't change:**
- Validation still requires human judgment
- Final decisions still require accountability
- Trust still requires human relationships
- Change management still requires human empathy

**Our response:**
- Humans become "validators" and "decision makers"
- Focus shifts to higher-value activities
- Consulting becomes even more critical

---

### Scenario 3: Full AGI (Unlikely in 10 years)

**What changes:**
- AI can do most technical work
- AI can interact with stakeholders
- AI can make some decisions

**What doesn't change:**
- Legal accountability (humans sign contracts)
- Ultimate decision authority (humans own businesses)
- High-stakes decisions (humans want human judgment)
- Relationship preference (humans prefer human advisors)

**Our response:**
- Pivot fully to strategic advisory
- Focus on highest-value activities
- Leverage AI as tool, not replacement
